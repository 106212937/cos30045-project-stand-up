Beyond the Crash: The Hidden Burden of Road Injuries in Australia

While road fatalities dominate headlines, tens of thousands of Australians are admitted to hospital every year from non-fatal crashes. Explore the 2011–2021 BITRE dataset to uncover who is most at risk and why raw counts only tell half the story.

1. A Decade of Trauma: National Hospitalisation Trends (2011–2021)

National traffic hospitalisations climbed steadily from 34,270 in 2012 to a peak of 40,060 in 2019, before dropping sharply in 2020 during COVID-19 travel restrictions and rebounding to 39,505 in 2021.

Data Governance Note: Victoria changed admission criteria in July 2012 to exclude Emergency-Department-only stays, and NSW made a similar change in June 2017. Longitudinal comparisons across these structural breaks should be interpreted with caution.

2. The Remoteness Paradox (2021)

Major Cities record the highest raw volume of injuries due to population size. Toggle to Rate per 100k Population to see how per-capita risk reverses in Remote Australia.

3. Disproportionate Risk: Road User Share (2021)

While car drivers account for the largest single group (33.7%), motorcyclists (22.5%) and pedal cyclists (20.7%) together represent over 43% of all traffic hospitalisations.

4. Demographic Profile: Hospitalisations by Age Cohort (2021)

In raw numbers, the broader 40–64 age bracket records the highest count (13,026 cases) simply because it spans 25 years of population. However, young adults aged 17–25 face the steepest per-capita hospitalisation rate in Australia.

Data Source: Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE) & Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (NHMD), 2011–2021.
